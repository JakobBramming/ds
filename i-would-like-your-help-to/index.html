Master's Thesis · Operations Research

Vehicle routing with dynamic patient transport requests

Built a simulation and optimization model to study how patient time window length affected operational efficiency, internal driver utilization, and patient excess ride time in a real transport setting. The historical data was large, with approximately 2,000 rides per day split between pre-planned tasks and requests arriving dynamically during the day.

I replicated real-life operational decisions as closely as possible, simulated the model on historical days, and compared combinations of objective functions for the static and dynamic routing problems. The best-performing setup was then used for sensitivity analysis on narrower and wider patient time windows.
